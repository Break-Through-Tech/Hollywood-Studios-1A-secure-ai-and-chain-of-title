Design proposal · September 2026

The same app, dressed for the room it's in.

HSI sells a private studio network and a secure creative OS to people who make film and television. Right now it wears a navy-and-gold brand on the front door and a stock SaaS template inside. This proposes one system for both — no feature changes, every screen re-set in it.

Scope visual only Audited 9 routes, 2 widths, 8,899 lines of CSS, 40 components This page is set in the proposed system
01

Why it feels old

"Old" here is not age — the code is months old. It is the feeling of a template: decisions that were never made, so defaults made them. Ten specific ones, with evidence.

Two identities in one product

The brand is navy and gold — logo, hero, login. The app is Tailwind's indigo and blue. In 8,899 lines of CSS the brand gold appears three times; the off-brand indigo and blue appear forty-two. A member walks through a gold door into someone else's dashboard.

One typeface doing every job

Urbanist is a geometric display face — round, friendly, made for posters. It is set at every size from 9px hashes to 100px headlines, in nine weights. Small UI text in it reads soft; large headlines read like a startup deck. Thirty distinct sizes are in use, including 11.5, 12.5 and 13.5.

30 font sizes13px ×13812px ×7811px ×479 weights loaded

No scale — for radius, spacing, or buttons

Twenty border radii between 1px and 60px. Six different primary buttons: navy pill (landing), blue pill (Creative OS "New"), indigo square (Settings), black square (Save), gold pill (Featured), lime pill (Apply). Two of them put #111 text on indigo and blue, which fails contrast. Nothing tells a member which action matters.

20 radius valuesradius 100px ×48 · 999px ×11 · 8px ×54 · 10px ×45.set-btn-primary: #111 on #4f46e5.vault-new-btn: #111 on #3b82f6

Everything is a card

White card, 1px border, 24px padding, 16px radius — on a white page. Hierarchy comes from boxes rather than type, so nothing leads. The profile is a card inside a card; the Home page is seven cards of equal weight, and the feed is one of them.

.net-card / .prof-section / .vault-card / .ppl-card / .dm-* — same recipe

Islands

People is a dark navy page with a constellation background — under a light gray sidebar. The landing is white with a dark hero. Settings is a light modal over 70% black. Each was designed on its own; nothing was designed as a set.

.ppl-page background #1a2640.net-sidebar background rgba(245,245,247)

No app on a phone

Below 768px the sidebar is display: none and nothing replaces it: a member on a phone has no way to reach Search, Projects, People or Messages. The Home top bar overflows to the right; the profile page scrolls sideways.

global.css:4202 .net-sidebar { display:none }no bottom nav, no menu

Placeholders show through

Broken avatar images render their alt text ("Rob", "TJ O'D…"). Initials sit in flat indigo circles. "Known For" shows five empty gray tiles for a person whose posters are in the database. Analytics is a "Coming Soon" page in the main nav. Each one tells a member the product is unfinished, when it is the presentation that is.

Style the stylesheet can't reach

418 inline style={{…}} attributes across the components, so a token change in CSS misses them. Six !important. One :focus-visible rule in the whole app, no prefers-reduced-motion, and roughly 170 transition: all. The hover on nav icons is scale(1.05).

style={{ ×418!important ×6:focus-visible ×1transition ×172

The landing page is three landing pages

The hero is the strongest thing in the product. Below it: a lime highlighter (#DEFF9B) that appears nowhere else, "Hollywood StudiosTM" with a literal TM, three sections of dark navy cards in three different styles, and scroll-reveal that leaves a section invisible if its observer never fires — which is exactly what a link preview or a screenshot sees.

.phrase-highlight #DEFF9B.cta-btn lime.reveal { opacity: 0 }

What's working and should survive: the hero's night-lot photograph and its gold italic; the hover-expanding rail is a good instinct; the Creative OS project page's structure (title, badges, tabs, side panel); the featured-card and coverage layouts.

02

Call sheet, not dashboard

The industry already has a visual language for serious paper: the call sheet, the coverage report, the one-sheet, the screenplay page. Quiet, dense, precise, with one thing that matters marked in gold. That is the register HSI should sit in.

One ink, one gold

Navy is the working color — text, actions, links. Gold appears once per screen, for the thing that earned it: the featured cohort, a verified timestamp, the upgrade. Never for decoration.

Type does the hierarchy

A display face for names and titles, a workhorse for everything else, the screenplay face for scripts. Boxes are for things — a person, a project — not for sections.

Two surfaces, one shadow

Cool paper for the page, white for the surface, hairlines between. Shadow only on things that float: menus, modals, the phone tab bar.

The industry's own conventions

Posters at 2:3. Credits as a block with years in a tabular column. Scripts in Courier. Coverage verdicts as PASS / CONSIDER / RECOMMEND. Timestamps as hashes, set in mono.

Same app on a phone

The rail becomes a tab bar under 768px. Every page has a phone layout, checked, not assumed.

Light, with a dark option

Reading scripts and long bios wants a light surface. Tokens are written so a dark theme is a switch, not a rewrite — this page is that switch working.

06

How to ship it

Foundation first, then one surface at a time, each on its own branch and each screenshot-checked at 1440 and 390 before it merges. Nothing changes what a page does.

Phase 1≈ 2 days

Foundation

Tokens, fonts, and the primitives every page uses — without touching page layouts yet. The app looks 40% better on this phase alone because buttons, inputs, chips and the rail are on every screen.

  • Replace :root with the token set; load Bricolage Grotesque, Instrument Sans, Courier Prime, JetBrains Mono; retire Urbanist.
  • One button family (.btn + variants) replacing the six; one input, chip, tab, seal, avatar.
  • Rail with labels and the signed-in person; tab bar under 768px.
  • Split global.css into tokens.css, primitives.css, shell.css and one file per surface — same cascade, findable.
src/styles/index.htmlAppSidebar.tsxAppLayout.tsx
Phase 2≈ 3 days

Network

The surfaces members see first: Home, People, Profile, Projects and the entity pages. Inline styles move into classes as each file is touched.

  • Home feed as the page; requests and featured in the side column.
  • People to light; featured card and directory on the new components.
  • Profile header, Known For with posters, filmography credits; entity page one-sheet and cast block.

Rules for the whole run

  • No functionality change. Routes, API calls, state and copy stay; only markup classes and CSS move. Where a component's structure has to change for layout (the profile header, the rail), the props and handlers don't.
  • Inline styles retire as files are touched, not in a separate sweep — 418 is too many to do blind, and each one is obvious in context.
  • Every phase ends with screenshots at 1440 and 390 of every route it touched, checked against the mockups here, before it merges. The headless-Chrome script from this audit does that in one command.
  • Accessibility comes with the primitives: a visible focus ring, prefers-reduced-motion, 4.5:1 on all text, and no transition: all.
  • Dark theme is a token swap and ships when Phase 4 is done, behind the existing Settings page.

Three small things surfaced by the audit that are bugs rather than design, and worth fixing on the way: broken avatar images should fall back to initials; IMDb-sourced profiles show a Wikidata attribution line; Known For never renders the posters that seeded titles have.

07

Your calls

Four things I'd rather you decide than assume. Each has a recommendation; none blocks Phase 1 except the first.

Typeface

Bricolage Grotesque + Instrument Sans as proposed, or keep Urbanist and change everything else. Keeping Urbanist keeps most of the "template" feeling; it is the single biggest tell.

Recommend: change the faces.

People page

Light like the rest of the app (as mocked), or keep it as the one dark room. Dark works for a marquee moment; it costs consistency, and the constellation canvas will fight the portraits.

Recommend: light.

Dark theme

Light only, or light with a dark toggle. The tokens make dark cheap; the question is whether to test and maintain two.

Recommend: light first, dark after Phase 4.

Landing page

Part of this run (Phase 5), or a separate marketing project later. It shares the type and tokens either way.

Recommend: last in this run — keep the hero, re-set the rest.
